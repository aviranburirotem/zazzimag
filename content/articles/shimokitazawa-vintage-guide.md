Shimokitazawa, or "Shimokita" to locals, sits in Tokyo's Setagaya ward, where the Odakyu and Keio Inokashira lines cross. It's a short ride from Shinjuku on the Odakyu Line, but it feels like a different city: low buildings, narrow streets and a dense mix of cafes, bars, small theatres, live music venues and second-hand clothing shops.

## Why vintage ended up here

Tokyo magazine DEEPTOKYO links Shimokita's vintage culture to its music and theatre scene. For decades, live houses such as SHELTER and CLUB Que and small stages like those in Suzunari Yokocho have drawn musicians, actors and other creative people to the area. That crowd made the neighbourhood open to self-expression, and shop owners followed. Many stores here are built around what the owner loves rather than what's trending.

## Shops worth knowing

These are the stores named in recent guides from DEEPTOKYO and Yokogao Magazine. Small shops change, so check before you go.

- **Flamingo** has been in Shimokita since 2005 and runs two stores focused on mid-century American vintage.
- **Pheasant** reflects its owner's love of motorcycles and rock music: leather jackets, denim and workwear.
- **sowhat vintage** specialises in vintage caps with company logos and movie references.
- **Abesho 2nd** is full of American vintage accessories, more than 1,000 of them according to DEEPTOKYO.
- **e's yarn** is a calmer, neatly organised shop for classic American casual pieces, mostly womenswear.
- **MIMIC** mixes anime T-shirts with rare designer vintage.
- **2nd STREET** is a large resale chain. Yokogao puts most of its prices between ¥4,000 and ¥10,000.
- **Stick Out** prices every item at ¥800, according to Yokogao.

## A neighbourhood under construction

In March 2013 the Odakyu tracks through the area were moved underground, which freed up the land above them for new projects. One of them, Reload, opened in June 2021 along the old line with more than 20 independent shops laid out like a street rather than a mall. Under the tracks near the station, Mikan Shimokita adds bookshops, food and entertainment. The new buildings sit right next to the older backstreets, so a walk through Shimokita still mixes old and new.
