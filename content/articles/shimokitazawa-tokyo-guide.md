Just seven minutes from Shinjuku by train, Shimokitazawa, affectionately known as Shimokita, is a neighbourhood shaped by vintage fashion, live music and independent culture. Its narrow streets weave between second-hand shops, intimate theatres, cafés and creative spaces, making it a favourite for vintage lovers and street-style enthusiasts.

## Shops to explore

- **Flamingo** — American and European vintage, from graphic tees and classic denim to distinctive accessories.
- **STEP AHEAD** — American casualwear, streetwear and second-hand vintage staples.
- **American Joe** — A second-hand store where visitors can sell pre-loved clothes, giving garments a second life and supporting circular fashion.
- **RAGTAG** — Pre-owned designer fashion from Japanese and international labels, spanning contemporary and established names.
- **PEEL by BIG APPLE STORE** — American vintage and collectible casualwear, including vintage Ralph Lauren, graphic sweatshirts and standout outerwear.

![Inside STEP AHEAD in Shimokitazawa, with racks of second-hand clothing and casual vintage styles](/images/articles/shimokitazawa-tokyo-guide/sihmokita-article-9-2.jpg "Streetwear staples and vintage casualwear at STEP AHEAD, where everyday pieces meet individual style  | Photo by Netta Meisles")

- **Shimokitazawa Flea Market** — Held at Shimokita Senrogai Akichi, a short walk from the station, this open-air market brings together independent vendors selling vintage clothing, handmade accessories, ceramics and artwork. Entry is free, and the changing selection makes each visit different.
- **KIMONO ARCH Shimokitazawa** — A contemporary take on Japanese dress, featuring designer kimonos made with original, eco-friendly textiles and modern silhouettes.


![A person wearing a contemporary kimono outside KIMONO ARCH in Shimokitazawa, Tokyo](/images/articles/shimokitazawa-tokyo-guide/shimokitazawa-2-74-2.jpg "Traditional Japanese dress, reimagined. Contemporary kimono design meets an eco-conscious approach at KIMONO ARCH  | Photo by Netta Meisles ")


## Old streets, new spaces

Shimokitazawa’s newer developments offer distinct perspectives on urban life. **reload**, along the former Odakyu railway corridor, combines low-rise architecture, open walkways and independent boutiques in a design-led setting. **Bonus Track** has a neighbourhood-village feel, bringing together small shops, cafés, a shared gallery and community events. **Mikan Shimokita**, beneath the elevated Keio Inokashira Line, embraces an urban aesthetic, with restaurants, retail, books and entertainment integrated into the railway surroundings.

## Where to eat

Shimokitazawa’s food scene is as eclectic as its fashion. **Ming Teng HAO HAO**, inside reload, serves entirely vegan Taiwanese-inspired dishes, including mapo tofu rice, dumplings and *douhua*, a delicate soy-milk pudding.
 **Curry Spice Gelateria KALPASI** pairs aromatic curries with inventive gelato. For tacos, **TACOS 3Hermanos** brings Mexican street food to the neighbourhood, while **400℃ PIZZA Piu** serves creative pizzas in a contemporary setting. For something sweet, **FLIPPER’S** is known for its signature soufflé pancakes, served with toppings such as fresh strawberries and maple buttercream, alongside savoury brunch dishes.

![A serving of fluffy soufflé pancakes topped with fresh strawberries at FLIPPER’S](/images/articles/shimokitazawa-tokyo-guide/sihmokita-article-10.jpg "Cloud-soft soufflé pancakes topped with fresh strawberries at FLIPPER’S in Shimokitazawa  | Photo by Netta Meisles ")



From vintage discoveries and independent boutiques to inventive food and thoughtfully designed spaces, Shimokitazawa brings together the many sides of Tokyo’s creative culture. Its charm lies in the details: a tucked-away shop, a lively backstreet or an unexpected find around the corner.
