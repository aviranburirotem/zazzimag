Harajuku is known for its street fashion and independent boutiques. Its backstreets, known as Ura-Harajuku, became a hub for independent designers in the late 1980s, helping shape the area’s experimental style. Today, this spirit can be seen in fashion subcultures such as Lolita, with its Victorian-inspired dresses, and Decora, known for colorful outfits and playful accessories.

## Lolita Fashion: Victorian & Rococo Elegance



![Kinako Mochi wearing a detailed Lolita fashion outfit](/images/articles/harajuku-street-fashion-a-guide-to-lolita-and-decora/july-1.jpg "Kinako Mochi wearing a Lolita outfit | Photo by Netta Meisles")



Lolita fashion reimagines Victorian and Rococo aesthetics through structured silhouettes, voluminous petticoats, intricate lace, and theatrical details. The subculture branches into distinct directions: Sweet Lolita embraces pastels, whimsical prints, and doll-like innocence, while Gothic Lolita leans into dramatic tailoring, dark palettes, and melancholic romance.

- Where to explore: Laforet Harajuku (opened in 1978), the iconic multi-story haven for alternative Japanese fashion brands.



![Two Japanese girls wearing Sweet Lolita and Gothic Lolita fashion outfits](/images/articles/harajuku-street-fashion-a-guide-to-lolita-and-decora/j26-51-2.jpg "Two Japanese girls wearing Sweet Lolita and Gothic Lolita outfits | Photo by Netta Meisles")


## Decora Fashion: Color, Chaos & Maximalism


Emerging in the late 1990s, Decora is known for hyper-saturated colors, layered outfits, hair clips, and plastic toys. Rooted in a DIY spirit, the style turns everyday nostalgic items into wearable art, reflecting the horror vacui approach of filling every space with detail. Artist and art director Sebastian Masuda helped shape Harajuku’s visual culture through an art style that blends contemporary art, theater, and Decora’s bold aesthetic.


- Where to explore: 6%DOKIDOKI, the boutique Masuda opened in 1995, to discover his distinctive style and its connection to Decora.



![The 6%DOKIDOKI storefront in Harajuku, Tokyo, known for its colorful Decora fashion and accessories.](/images/articles/harajuku-street-fashion-a-guide-to-lolita-and-decora/pxl-20240605-075211436-enhanced-sr-1.jpg "The 6%DOKIDOKI storefront in Harajuku, Tokyo | Photo by Netta Meisles")



## Jirai Kei Fashion: Sweetness with an Edge



Jirai Kei is a contemporary Japanese fashion style that combines feminine details with a distinctive mix of sweetness and darker elements. Black and pink are common colors, but the style also appears in softer pastel palettes. Frills, lace, ribbons, bows, platform shoes and doll-like makeup are among its recognizable features.

Where to explore: Shibuya 109 is a good place to look for Jirai Kei-inspired fashion. The shopping mall includes brands such as MA*RS and LIZ LISA, which offer styles associated with this feminine, detail-rich aesthetic.

![Two Girls in Pastel Jirai Kei on the Streets of Shibuya](/images/articles/harajuku-street-fashion-a-guide-to-lolita-and-decora/shibuya-2-21-2.jpg "A sweet mix of pastel colours, playful details and Jirai Kei fashion, spotted on the streets of Shibuya | Photo by Netta Meisles")


## Gyaru Fashion: Bold Glamour & Individuality


Gyaru is a Japanese fashion subculture that emerged in the 1990s, known for its glamorous, expressive approach to personal style. Bold makeup, dramatic lashes, styled hair, statement nails and eye-catching outfits are among its signature elements. Over the years, Gyaru has evolved into a variety of styles, each with its own take on glamour and self-expression.

Where to explore: Shibuya 109 is closely associated with Gyaru culture and remains an important destination for Japanese youth fashion. For a more immersive experience, @ppgalclub in Shibuya offers Gyaru makeovers and photoshoots, giving visitors the chance to experience the style firsthand.
