Some artists create artworks. Others build entire universes around them. Mariko Mori certainly belongs to the latter. The Japanese artist brings together fashion, technology, history and spirituality, creating a visual world that seems to come from the distant future while exploring timeless questions about humanity and our place in the universe.



![The artwork “Mirror of Water” Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham](/images/articles/mariko-mori-all-that-shines-tokyo/article82033-3.jpg "The artwork “Mirror of Water” Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham")



Her new exhibition, *All That Shines*, brings together around 40 works spanning more than three decades, from her early career in the 1990s to the present day. It offers a special opportunity to follow the evolution of her artistic language through photography, video, sculpture and interactive installations, tracing the journey from the futuristic imagery of her early work to a deeper exploration of consciousness, nature and human existence.


![“All That Shines” exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham](/images/articles/mariko-mori-all-that-shines-tokyo/article82033-8.jpg "“All That Shines” exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham")



One of the most fascinating aspects of Mori’s practice is her research-driven approach. She explores ancient civilizations, archaeological sites and spiritual traditions, with a particular interest in Japan’s prehistoric Jōmon culture and ancient Celtic cultures. She connects historical discoveries and mythology with ideas from physics and astrophysics, creating an intriguing dialogue between human history and the cosmos. For Mori, the past and the future are part of the same search for a deeper understanding of existence and the connections that unite us.


![ The artwork “Flat Stone” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham](/images/articles/mariko-mori-all-that-shines-tokyo/article82033-6.jpg " The artwork “Flat Stone” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham")


Her visual language has undergone an equally fascinating transformation. In her early work, Mori used herself as a model, creating futuristic, otherworldly characters inspired by anime, fashion and Japanese pop culture. Bold, colourful costumes were an integral part of the artwork. 


![The artwork “Play with Me” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham](/images/articles/mariko-mori-all-that-shines-tokyo/article82033-2.jpg "The artwork “Play with Me” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham")


Over time, her aesthetic became more abstract and spiritual, embracing translucency, glass, polished metals and reflections of light. White plays a central role: white light contains every colour of the visible spectrum, and Mori explores the refraction and reflection of light to create works with an almost celestial presence. Her personal style follows the same philosophy; she has worn exclusively white since 1998, describing it as the colour closest to light.


![The artwork “Miracle” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham](/images/articles/mariko-mori-all-that-shines-tokyo/article82033-4.jpg "The artwork “Miracle” by Mariko Mori, featured in the All That Shines exhibition at the Mori Art Museum | Photo by Zorka Avraham")


One of the exhibition’s highlights is *Wave UFO*, an interactive installation in which visitors lie inside a spacecraft-like structure, wear sensors that read their brainwaves and watch their brain activity translated into visual imagery in real time. An extraordinary encounter between art, technology and consciousness.

**Why visit?**

*All That Shines* is an opportunity to step into the world of an artist who thinks on a cosmic scale, connecting art, science, archaeology and spirituality in unexpected ways. Her work invites us to reconsider our relationship with nature and our place in the universe. Make sure not to miss the *Wave UFO* experience: each session accommodates just three visitors, and daily capacity is limited. Registration takes place at the installation, with check-in opening at 10:00 and 14:00. Arriving early is highly recommended to improve your chances of taking part. **The exhibition is on view until March 28, 2027 | Mori Art Museum, Tokyo**
